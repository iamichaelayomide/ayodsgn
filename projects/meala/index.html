Product strategy, UI/UX & frontend case study

Meala

A personalized food subscription platform that helps people match chef-prepared meals to their goals, dietary preferences, and daily routine.

01

The challenge

Healthy eating services often make people choose between rigid meal plans and overwhelming menus. The experience can feel generic even when the customer has a clear fitness goal, calorie target, dietary preference, or allergy concern.

Meala needed to communicate premium food quality while making personalization and subscription planning feel understandable. The product also had to support discovery, comparison, customization, pricing, and conversion in one responsive journey.

How might meal planning feel personally calibrated without making every choice feel like work?
02

Strategy and positioning

I framed Meala around a simple promise: chef-prepared meals matched to the person, not a generic weekly box. That positioning gave the product a useful balance of performance, convenience, and culinary quality.

The content strategy organized the page around the questions a prospective subscriber would naturally ask: How does it work? Can it match my goals? What makes the food different? Which meals are available? What will it cost? Can I trust the service?

03

A collaborative AI-assisted workflow

I used ChatGPT for strategic framing and ideation: clarifying the product story, exploring customer concerns, organizing the experience, and pressure-testing the information architecture.

I moved into Figma for moodboarding, visual exploration, responsive UI design, and component decisions. Once the experience and visual direction were resolved, I used Antigravity to build out the frontend, then refined the result against the Figma intent.

04

The experience architecture

The landing experience starts with the outcome—personalized healthy meals—then progressively reveals how the service works. A goal-based meal customizer lets people choose an objective, energy target, meal frequency, and dietary preferences before seeing calibrated macros and recommended dishes.

The rest of the journey supports decision-making with a side-by-side value comparison, ingredient principles, a rotating menu, clear subscription plans, member stories, and practical FAQs.

05

Key interface systems

The meal customizer turns complex nutrition inputs into a guided sequence. People can adjust goals, calories, frequency, and preferences while immediately seeing an understandable per-meal target.

Menu cards combine appetite appeal with useful nutrition detail. Calories, protein, dietary tags, preparation time, and pricing remain scannable without crowding out the food.

Subscription plans use a consistent comparison structure. Meal count, weekly price, per-meal value, benefits, and flexibility are grouped so customers can compare plans without decoding different layouts.

06

From design to build

The build preserved the bold editorial hierarchy from Figma while adding working interactions: a meal carousel, goal and macro controls, dietary filters, menu categories, plan selection, a meal box, testimonials, and responsive navigation.

The main implementation challenge was keeping a long, content-rich page energetic without losing orientation. Repeated section labels, strong headings, consistent cards, and clear actions create rhythm while responsive layouts protect readability on smaller screens.

07

Reflection

Meala showed how AI can support a design process without replacing design judgment. Strategy prompts accelerated exploration, Figma remained the place where hierarchy and visual taste were resolved, and the AI-assisted build made it possible to test the complete experience as a working system.

What worked

The strongest result is the connection between personalization and appetite appeal: the product feels useful enough for performance-minded customers without turning food into a sterile spreadsheet.

Next

The next phase would validate the customizer with target customers, test plan comprehension, connect real checkout and account flows, and measure where visitors need more reassurance.
